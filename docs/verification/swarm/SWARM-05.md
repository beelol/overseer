# SWARM-05 — serial versus parallel benefit

Status: partial. Revision: `87fee8f`. Support level: fixture-only, committed benefit decisions and admission gate.

Input: two paired plans estimate the same two independent jobs. Each plan supplies planning, context transfer, worker execution, retry allowance, integration and review costs in elapsed milliseconds and native quota units. The fixture compares a 240 ms serial plan with a 210 ms parallel plan, 40 milli-points of allocation and 10 milli-points reserved to finish. Variants increase parallel context cost to remove the time benefit, reduce finishing reserve, exceed total allocation, make the jobs dependent, or make both plans unaffordable. Another fixture removes a phase estimate, changes a job identity, or supplies an uncalibrated worker estimate. Admission fixtures exercise no decision, committed serial and parallel decisions, daemon restart, changed decisions while workers are active, job IDs outside the batch, and a second batch after two workers submit accepted evidence and exit. Existing 32-worker, review-pressure, scheduler, dispatch, context and outage fixtures now declare synthetic beneficial batches when they expect concurrent admission.

Expected: choose parallel only for independent jobs with a strict time benefit, affordable total usage and enough finishing capacity. Prefer serial on ties. Reject incomplete or inconsistent estimates. An unproven run must not fan out; a committed decision must survive restart and bound the specific batch without bypassing quota, worker or global ceilings.

Observed: `swarm.benefit.preview` selects the two-worker parallel plan with a recorded 30 ms expected gain, 35 milli-points total usage and 10 milli-points finishing cost. It returns serial when coordination erases the benefit, reserve or allocation is insufficient, or jobs are dependent; it reports blocked when neither path is affordable. Malformed estimates fail. A fixture-only `swarm.benefit.commit` persists the effective estimate and decision by plan revision and wave. With no decision, the second active worker is blocked. A serial decision holds it too; a parallel decision admits only estimated jobs up to its recorded batch size, subject to the existing quota and concurrency checks. The decision and replay survive daemon restart. A changed decision cannot replace an active batch; after accepted workers exit, the next ready batch receives wave 2 and is admitted. The four focused tests and full offline Rust suite pass (147 tests: 10 unit, 48 protocol, 89 Swarm).

Commands: `cargo test --offline -p overseerd --test swarm_benefit -- --nocapture`; `cargo test --workspace --offline -q`.

Evidence: `daemon/tests/swarm_benefit.rs`, `daemon/src/swarm/benefit.rs`, `daemon/src/swarm/admission.rs`, `daemon/tests/swarm_admission.rs`, `daemon/tests/swarm_runtime.rs`.

Remaining: only the fixture path commits these decisions. The estimator trusts caller-supplied costs, independence and allocation figures; real plan/resource independence and fresh shared-account headroom are not yet inputs. No live director produces estimates, and actual elapsed/usage outcomes are not retained for calibration. The no-decision path permits one worker alongside the director, rather than a fully qualified single-agent fallback. Auto Mode and the shared reservation authority must supply live data before normal scheduling uses this gate. The RFC criterion stays unchecked.
