# SWARM-51 — uncertain worker liveness

Status: partial. Revision: `8a72059`. Fixture: supervised local generic worker running `/bin/sleep` with a live quota reservation.

Input: admit and launch one worker, then inject Overseer's `disconnected` run state with an `ended_ms` value while its process may still be alive. Call worker reconciliation, allow the daemon's periodic terminal scan to run, and call reconciliation again. Restore the ordinary running state before the rest of the restart/stop fixture proceeds.

Expected: a lost supervisor or transport is not confirmed process death. No terminal event, attempt finalization, reservation release or replacement eligibility may follow solely from the `disconnected` status or its timestamp.

Observed: before `8a72059`, `swarm.worker.reconcile` returned `terminal` for the disconnected worker and confirmed its exit. The reconciler now returns `unknown`, and the periodic terminal scan excludes disconnected runs. The attempt remains `registered` and its quota reservation remains `active` through a daemon tick.

Follow-up revision `1d5792d`: the daemon samples up to four due local worker control sockets per tick, with a 250 ms probe timeout and 15-second per-worker cadence. The shared durable reducer stores `reachable`, `suspect`, and `unknown`: the first failed probe starts an unreachable clock; 60 seconds of continuous failures marks unknown; a successful probe clears it. The scripted worker emits no output yet is sampled as reachable. A fixture injects repeated failed probes around the exact threshold and a progress message in between; progress does not reset the clock or create another attempt. Out-of-order samples are rejected. Reconciliation reports the sampled unknown state, and stored state survives daemon restart. The focused regression and full Rust suite pass (128 tests: 9 unit, 48 protocol, 71 Swarm).

Follow-up revision `442443a`: the supervised worker's actual Unix control socket is moved while its process stays alive. The fixture advances the existing daemon probe through five 15-second intervals using a test-only clock hook. Each probe fails through the real local transport path; the state becomes `suspect`, then `unknown` at 60 seconds, and reconciliation preserves uncertainty. Restoring the socket makes the next probe `reachable` without a replacement attempt. The focused regression and full Rust suite pass (128 tests). This is a deterministic clock replay, not a 60-second wall-clock outage.

Follow-up revision `f91fa58`: a focused red regression showed that direct attempt finalization accepted `disconnected` plus `ended_ms` as confirmed exit even though reconciliation did not. Finalization now rejects that state, leaving the attempt and reservation intact. A supervised-worker deadline fixture sends ten progress messages during the original 2.5-second deadline, then observes deadline stop, non-successful worker exit, a cancelled job, and no extra attempt. The focused fixtures and full Rust suite pass (128 tests).

Follow-up revision `6359926`: an admitted job now stores an absolute deadline, defaulting to 15 minutes from admission and capped at the run deadline; a fixture admission can request a shorter bounded duration. A daemon tick durably requests a stop for an overdue job, retries interrupting its linked process until it exits, and leaves a disconnected process unconfirmed. After confirmed exit, a deadline-stopped job is `failed`, not retried or accepted. Code guards also reject late launch and director acceptance before the next timer tick. The two-worker fixture sends five progress messages, then observes the short-deadline worker fail while its sibling and the run remain active; the default-deadline fixture checks the stored value. The focused tests and full Rust suite pass (129 tests: 9 unit, 48 protocol, 72 Swarm).

Follow-up revision `a7f229b`: a fixture-only fault seam persists the due job's `cancel_requested` state and one stop envelope while withholding the external worker interrupt. The test kills the daemon at that point, restarts it before the real 4-second job deadline, and observes the periodic retry interrupt the original supervised worker. The worker exits unsuccessfully, the job becomes `failed` with `job_deadline`, the attempt count stays one, and the durable stop envelope remains unique. The focused test was red before the seam existed; after implementation, the focused test and full offline Rust suite pass (130 tests: 9 unit, 48 protocol, 73 Swarm). This exercises daemon-crash recovery around the timeout side effect; it does not emulate a worker that ignores interrupt.

Follow-up revision `9d00d2b`: a supervised Python fixture ignores SIGINT through the short job deadline. After the daemon requests interrupt, the same worker remains running, the job stays `cancel_requested` with `job_deadline`, and no additional attempt or successful result appears. The fixture then kills that exact child process and observes an unsuccessful terminal state and a failed job. The focused test, seven Swarm runtime integration tests, and the full offline Rust suite pass (131 tests: 9 unit, 48 protocol, 74 Swarm). This verifies conservative deadline state while interrupt is ignored; it does not prove forced exit for a worker that also ignores later escalation.

Follow-up at `12ef1b6`: under one full-suite run, the worker-restart fixture observed `suspect` immediately after launch, before the new supervisor socket was ready.
